## Problem Statement
Regentify drills students with exam practice questions ("testing" mode) but has no dedicated feature to help students learn/understand material before being tested. The founder (a CS teacher) suspects this gap exists based on his daughter's offhand comment that the app is "good for testing not learning."

## Demand Evidence
Weak and secondhand: founder's daughter (Geometry, Earth Science, Physical Setting/Space) made the comment once; founder admits "maybe it's not 100% true" and can't recall a specific triggering moment (no bad grade, no remembered confusion episode cited). This is a hypothesis, not validated behavior — explicitly flagged during the session, not glossed over.

## Status Quo
Daughter currently combines Khan Academy (for learning) with Regentify (for practice testing). Founder's core observation: Khan Academy requires sitting at a desk/Chromebook — it's desk-bound, video-based learning, not usable in dead time. Landscape check confirmed Khan Academy content is not specifically aligned to NY Regents exams (generic Common Core/SAT-oriented), reinforcing that existing "learning" content isn't tailored to this exam.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
Two concrete populations, both accessible to the founder directly:
1. Students with long bus commutes (~1 hour) — dead time currently unused for studying.
2. Students who love gamification/competition (leaderboards) — already engaged by Regentify's existing XP/streak/leaderboard infrastructure.

Founder teaches CS at a high school; his students already use the app for practice. He has teacher-friends who teach the actual Regents subjects (Geometry, Earth Science) and can pilot with real students — this access, not the product idea itself, is the real asset.

Narrowest wedge: don't build a new mode yet. Test whether a teaching step (a short explanation) added to existing test-mode questions changes anything measurable, before investing in a new screen/architecture.

## Premises
1. Students have a workaround (Khan Academy) that's generic and desk-bound — not Regents-specific, not mobile-native.
2. Demand evidence is weak (one secondhand comment), but the founder has a cheap, fast way to get real evidence: his own classroom plus teacher-friends who teach the actual subjects.
3. **(Revised after cross-model challenge, then revised again during engineering review)** The wedge is NOT simply mobile/dead-time delivery of existing questions. Gamified bite-sized questions without any explanation are not "learning" — they're testing in smaller pieces with a streak counter.
   - **Correction:** `shared/content/geometry/questions.js` already has an `explanation` field per question, and `mobile/src/screens/QuizScreen.jsx` already shows it **after** the student answers (full version Premium-gated). So "add explanations" is not the actual gap — explanations already exist.
   - **Refined wedge:** the untested variable is **timing** — does showing the "why" *before* the student attempts the question (pre-teaching) change 24h retention, versus the existing post-answer feedback model? This is what the pilot now tests.

## Cross-Model Perspective

**Codex cold read:** Steelmanned the idea as "Regentify becomes a Regents-specific pocket tutor: five-minute sessions that teach one concept, guide a student through an example, then check whether they can apply it independently." Flagged the Khan Academy comparison (Q2) as the most revealing answer — concrete and falsifiable, unlike the daughter's vague quote. Challenged premise 3 directly: instructional quality matters more than delivery packaging; existing exam questions are assessment material, not a teaching sequence. Proposed test: does next-day transfer improve only when sessions include worked examples and reduced guidance? Recommendation: build and classroom-test one teacher-reviewed teaching sequence, because real student access is the strongest asset while bus-ride demand remains unproven.

**Claude subagent cold read:** Independently reached the same challenge to premise 3 — a gamified stream of "here's a question, here's the answer" is not conceptually different from existing testing mode. Proposed a concrete two-variant pilot: (A) existing questions in bite-size streaks, no explanation, vs. (B) same chunking + a 1-2 sentence "why/how" before each item — measured via a 24-hour retention check. Recommendation: run this pilot before writing production code, since the whole feature bet collapses if chunking-without-explanation is indistinguishable from testing mode.

Both models converged independently on the same core risk — a strong, unprompted signal.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Pilot-First Probe (chosen)
Inject a short pre-answer "why" hint under a subset of existing test-mode questions for one Geometry topic (Congruence & Transformations, ids 3001-3006); A/B against the existing unchanged flow (question → answer → existing post-answer explanation). Measure via a 24-hour follow-up retention question. No new screens, no new nav, no changes to `shared/content/`. Draft pilot content: `docs/designs/learn-mode-pilot-content.md` (needs teacher-friend validation before running).
Effort: S. Risk: Low.

### Approach B: Minimal Learn Tab
New `LearnScreen` tab, one subject (Geometry), 3-5 hand-authored micro-lessons (explanation → worked example → guided problem w/ hints → independent check), separate streak from test mode. Ships to the classroom within days.
Effort: M. Risk: Med.

### Approach C: Structured Lesson Schema
New `lessons` content type in `shared/content/` generalized across subjects, with a generic step-renderer screen. Scales cleanly but is a large upfront investment with no validation yet.
Effort: L. Risk: High. **Rejected during session** — premature architecture before the teaching-step premise is even validated.

## Recommended Approach
Approach A (Pilot-First Probe). Rationale: the founder has free, fast, real classroom access — the cheapest possible way to test the one premise (explanation vs. no explanation) both independent AI reviews flagged as unresolved. Building Approach B or C before this test risks shipping a new tab that's functionally identical to existing testing mode under a different name.

## Open Questions
- Does the "why" micro-explanation actually move 24h retention, or is dead-time delivery format alone sufficient? (This is the entire question Approach A is designed to answer.)
- If Approach A shows a lift, which subject/topic set expands next — Geometry only, or does Earth Science/Space get added immediately?
- Founder has not yet confirmed the teacher-friends are willing/available to pilot this in their actual Regents classrooms (assumed access, not confirmed access).

## Success Criteria
- Approach A pilot runs for at least 1-2 weeks with real students in the founder's CS classroom and at least one teacher-friend's Regents subject classroom.
- Retention on the "explanation" variant is measurably (not just anecdotally) better than the no-explanation variant on a same-concept follow-up question 24 hours later.
- If no measurable difference: premise 3 is falsified as stated, and format alone (not teaching content) may be the real lever — revisit before building Approach B.

## Dependencies
- Confirm teacher-friend availability/willingness to pilot in their classrooms.
- No new content pipeline needed — hand-write explanations for one Geometry topic only.

## Engineering Review Notes (from /plan-eng-review)
- **Where the explanation text lives:** Keep it out of `shared/content/` for this pilot. `shared/content/` is the single source of truth flowing to both mobile and chromebook — don't add an `explanation` field there for 5-10 throwaway pilot questions. Instead, keep a small local/client-side mapping (question ID → explanation string) scoped to the pilot only. Promote to `shared/content/` only if the pilot shows a lift and Approach B gets greenlit.
- **Variant assignment (A vs. B):** Don't build an experimentation framework for a 5-10 question pilot. Use a simple deterministic split — hash of user ID mod 2, or have the teacher manually split the class roster in half. Either is sufficient at this scale.
- **Offline resilience:** The app is already offline-first Firestore — confirm the pilot's answer/retention tracking reuses that path rather than adding a new network call, since school wifi during a live class period is a realistic failure point.
- **What "testing" means here:** This is a manual classroom probe, not shipped code — skip formal unit/E2E tests. What's actually needed is a data-collection checklist: (student token, variant, question ID, correct/incorrect, timestamp) plus the 24h follow-up result per student.

## The Assignment
A draft topic (Congruence & Transformations) and draft pre-answer hints are in `docs/designs/learn-mode-pilot-content.md` — but before running the pilot, take that draft to your teacher-friend and have them confirm or replace it with what their students are actually stuck on right now. That judgment call has to come from a real teacher watching real students, not from a draft written without seeing the classroom. Then run the two-variant test (pre-answer hint vs. existing post-answer-only flow) this week. Don't build a new screen until you have that result.

## Landscape (search, 2026-11-07)

- Layer 1: pretesting (attempt before instruction) and worked examples are both established; which wins depends on whether the outcome is recall or transfer, and on the delay ([Miller 2024](https://michellemillerphd.substack.com/p/r3-29-may-28-2024-pretestings-surprising), [van Gog et al.](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/bitstream/handle/1874/327549/Testing.pdf?sequence=1), [Springer 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10212-024-00869-8)).
- Layer 2: Regents Geometry competitors are drill banks of past MC questions ([Geometry Regents Review](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1455778981), [Regents Prep 2025-2026](https://apps.apple.com/app/id857889651), [OpenExamPrep](https://open-exam-prep.com/practice/ny-regents-geometry)). None teach before testing. The gap the founder sensed is real in the market.
- Layer 3: a one-sentence hint before the question is neither a pretest nor a worked example; it is closer to a cue. The literature suggests the transfer item (the 24h follow-up) matters more than the hint wording, which supports the teacher's note to make the follow-up a proof-reason item.
